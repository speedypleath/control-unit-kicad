Panel Loom Wiring Guide

The other half of the build: what goes into each JST connector on the control-unit perfboard. The board wiring guide stops at the connector pads — this one starts there and runs out to the buttons, joysticks, numpad and sensor modules on the panel.

Before you crimp anything

Four conventions hold across every connector on this panel. Get these right and each individual cable below is mechanical.

Every switch is wired common-to-GND. No switch on this panel gets power. One terminal goes to the connector's GND pin, the other to its signal pin. The firmware holds every input with an internal pull-up, so an open switch reads HIGH and a closed one reads LOW. Momentary NO microswitches are non-polar — which of the two terminals you use for which does not matter.

Every LED is active HIGH. Anode to the connector's LED pin, cathode to its GND pin. The series resistors (R1–R5, 220 Ω) are already on the board, so the LED you fit must be a bare LED — not a pre-wired 5V or 12V illuminated button assembly with its own internal resistor, which will not light from 3.3 V through another 220 Ω.

Pin 1 is the pin listed first in the board guide's table for that connector. Don't rely on a housing marking to tell you which end is which — go back to the board wiring guide, read the grid ref for pin 1, and find that hole. Every table below numbers the board connector's pins, not the device's.

Pin numbers changed on 2026-08-23, and again for 26 of the 40 signals on 2026-08-24. Every connector signal was moved to whichever Teensy pin is physically closest to it on the board, so the assignments are no longer contiguous blocks (B1–B8 are not D20–D27 any more, the numpad is not D28–D35, and so on). Cables and connector pinouts are unchanged — only the firmware pin constants need updating. B1–B8 are the one exception to "nearest pin": they are pinned to the Teensy's D0–D12/D24–D32 header row and assigned in ascending order along the physical button row, so B1 gets the lowest number and B5 (the rightmost button) the highest, and no two button wires cross. The 2026-08-24 pass fixed a tie-break bug that let J1's pins land on physically crossing wires, and regenerating from that fix also picked up a shorter worst-case wire everywhere else (the map had drifted from what the generator script actually produces) — between the two, IRQ1/IRQ2 (swapped), IRQ4, B5, B8, CB3–CB5, all four J1 pins, J2.1/J2.2/J2.4, NP1.2–NP1.8, and LED2–LED5 moved. The map below is the current one; the schematic and both PCBs have not been resynced to it yet (tracked as a TODO in CLAUDE.md).

Cable stock: JST-XH 2.54 mm housings, 24–26 AWG stranded. Stranded, not solid — these runs flex every time the panel comes off.

B1–B8 — action buttons

Eight plain momentary buttons, two conductors each. B1–B4 are the left cluster, B5–B8 the right. They send MIDI notes 46–53 on channel 16, in that order.

CB1–CB5 — lit control buttons

Four conductors each: a switch and an LED sharing one housing, with a separate ground for each so a shorted lamp can't pull the switch line down with it. Notes 54–58 on channel 16. Each press also toggles that button's own lamp, so the panel shows mode state without the host having to drive it.

J1 / J2 — joysticks

Four-microswitch digital sticks, not analog: a common terminal and one normally-open switch per gate direction. J1 is the left stick, J2 the right.

The stick's own lead plugs in end-for-end. A typical 5-way arcade stick numbers its lead 1 common, 2 down, 3 up, 4 right, 5 left — common on pin 1. The board puts GND on pin 5. Crimping the lead reversed (its pin 1 onto board pin 5, its pin 2 onto board pin 4, and so on down) puts common on ground and lands left/right/up/down exactly on board pins 1–4, with no wires crossed inside the housing.

That is the loom this firmware assumes. If your stick numbers its lead differently, either re-crimp to match the table above or reorder kJoystickPins in lib/TeensyPanelCore/src/TeensyPanelCore.h — do not cross wires inside the housing, where nobody will find them later. Either way, push the stick in all four directions against the serial monitor before you close the panel.

NP1 — numpad

A 4×4 matrix on eight conductors and no ground at all. The firmware drives one row LOW at a time and reads the four columns with pull-ups, so the matrix supplies its own reference — there is nothing to connect to GND.

From a 10-way membrane tail, this is a straight-through crimp. That keypad's own tail runs 1 unused, 2–5 columns 0–3, 6–9 rows 0–3, 10 unused. Tail pin 2 goes to NP1 pin 1, tail pin 3 to NP1 pin 2, and so on to tail pin 9 on NP1 pin 8. Both unused ends are left off the housing.

NP1 pin 8 (row 3) sits on D13, the Teensy's onboard-LED pin. That is safe only because the matrix rows are driven outputs — the onboard LED and its resistor hang off D13 and would fight a pull-up input, so no input signal was allowed on that pin. The visible side effect is that the onboard LED flickers while the matrix is being scanned. Never move a column (pull-up input) onto D13.

Ring it out in resistance mode, not continuity mode. A membrane keypad's closed contact measures somewhere between 500 Ω and 10 kΩ — real conduction, but far too resistive to trip most multimeters' continuity beeper. A silent meter here means "membrane", not "open circuit".

S1–S6 — sensor modules

Six identical slots for Pico-based I²C sensor modules, on the six-pin Haptic Console Connector Standard v1.1. Every slot is wired the same, so a module can move between slots; only the IRQ line is per-slot.

Power the Pico from VSYS, never from 3V3. Connector pin 3 (+5V) goes to the Pico's VSYS; its own regulator makes the 3.3 V. Connector pin 2 (+3V3) is a reference rail only — leave it off the Pico entirely rather than back-feeding the Pico's regulator from both sides.

The bus pull-ups are already on the board. R12 and R13 (4.7 kΩ) pull SDA and SCL to +3V3 at the master end. Don't add another pair on a module, and never pull either line to 5 V — the Pico's GPIO is not 5 V tolerant.

v1.1 drops RESET and ID/ADDR from the original eight-pin standard. Nothing on this board can hard-reset a wedged module, and the Pico's ID/ADDR input (GP26) is left floating — so the idAdc field the Teensy prints in each packet is an unconnected-input reading, not a board identity. Read it as diagnostic noise until a board carries the resistor ladder.

Check before first power-on

The +5V rail is the Teensy's VIN pin. On USB power alone the whole console — Teensy, six Pico modules, every lamp — shares one 500 mA budget, which six modules will not fit inside. If you feed an external 5 V supply into VIN instead, cut the VUSB–VIN pads on the underside of the Teensy first; leaving them joined ties your bench supply to the host computer's USB rail.

Nothing on this panel should be closed at rest. Ring every switch out open before the connector goes on. The firmware's boot self-test prints all panel inputs open when it agrees with you, and names each offender when it doesn't.

Bring-up order

Plug in one family at a time. A panel that comes up all at once gives you no way to tell which loom is wrong.

  1. Power the Teensy over USB with every panel connector unplugged. Open the serial monitor at 115200 baud. The self-test should report all panel inputs open.
  2. Plug in B1–B8. Reset, confirm the self-test is still clean, then press each button and watch for B1 pressed … B8 released in order. A button that prints the wrong number is a swapped pair, not a firmware bug.
  3. Plug in CB1–CB5. Each press should print its legend (ARM, EN, SFT, CONF, ALT) and toggle that button's lamp.
  4. Plug in J1 and J2. Push each stick to all four gates and confirm the CC values move the way the table says: left 0, centre 64, right 127.
  5. Plug in NP1. Every digit key should print and send its note; A B C D * # print but have no note mapping yet. Watch row 3 in particular — see the pin 13 note above.
  6. Plug in one sensor module, on S1. Press s to rescan the bus, then i for the per-slot IRQ report — the slot you used should be the one counting up. Add the rest one slot at a time.

Serial commands: d toggles debug output, i prints the IRQ report, s rescans the I²C bus.
